# Pattern review for substantial edits

Read the passage in context before changing it. A feature is useful to flag when it delays the reader, blurs a claim, or repeats a move without a reason. It is never proof of authorship. For an unusually detailed audit, see [the extended contextual checks](extended-checks.md).

## First check what the reader receives

- **The answer arrives late.** A draft repeats the question, defines familiar terms, or builds a broad introduction before giving the requested fact or decision. Move the useful answer forward when the reader already has the background.
- **A paragraph adds no new step.** A claim is followed by a paraphrase of itself and a closing sentence that announces its importance. Keep the claim and the evidence or reasoning that supports it.
- **The wrong detail gets space.** A vague assertion receives several sentences while the actual measurement, example, instruction, or limitation receives one. Allocate words according to the material.
- **The reader must infer the link.** State what is being compared and why a result supports, weakens, or merely permits an inference. Do not replace an absent link with a dramatic transition.

## Then inspect repeated shapes

- Staged contrasts such as “not merely X but Y” can add force without adding information. State the positive claim directly unless the negative half answers a real misunderstanding.
- Repeated three-part lists, identical paragraph lengths, and a result–caution–summary ending in every section can make a long piece mechanical. Keep parallel form when the content itself is parallel.
- Stock openings, ritual signposts, and automatic final summaries often repeat what the heading or earlier paragraph already supplied. Remove them when they have no job.
- Repeated sentence openings, trailing participle clauses, and uniform sentence length can flatten pace. Rewrite around the information order rather than varying syntax by a fixed rule.
- Formatting can become a template: a heading for every short thought, bold labels on every paragraph, bullets for connected reasoning, or the same punctuation device in every sentence. Keep a format when it helps navigation or shows genuinely parallel items.

## Check words, claims, and voice

- Replace inflated importance, vague praise, and stock verbs with the actual action, measurement, or consequence. A technical term should stay when it names the correct concept.
- Avoid synonym cycling. Reusing a precise term is clearer than alternating among near-synonyms that subtly change its meaning.
- Name the actor where the source does. Use passive voice when the actor is unknown or the method/result should remain the subject.
- Separate observation, explanation, and cause. A possible mechanism must not become a measured result; a correlation must not become proof of cause.
- Keep calibrated uncertainty. Remove stacked hedges that obscure a single claim, but retain a hedge that marks a genuine limit in the evidence.
- A voice sample may show characteristic priorities, restraint, humor, or sentence movement. Match those features without borrowing its facts or constructing a personality the user did not supply.

## A short revision example

**Draft:** “It is important to note that the two groups showed a significant difference. This finding highlights the crucial role of temperature.”

**Revision when the supplied data give only a difference:** “The two groups differed at the measured temperatures. The data do not establish why.”

The revision removes announced importance and avoids inventing a causal explanation. If the source gives exact measurements or a tested mechanism, include those instead.

## False-positive guard

A single dash, a formal word, correct grammar, a list, a three-part statement, a passive sentence, first person, or an ordinary hedge is not an AI sign. The HC3 comparison summarized in [research basis](research-basis.md) found first person in many human-labelled answers and similar hedge rates in one technical subset. Genre can matter more than origin. Do not use a pattern catalog as an automatic rewrite list.
